Who we are

the voice of Hamilton’s African Diaspora Community at the municipal, provincial and federal levels

Hamilton Black Health Community Leaders Forum is the voice of Hamilton’s African Diaspora Community at the municipal, provincial and federal levels. The organization represents Hamilton’s African Communities before the city of Hamilton, and the provincial and federal governments in the spirit of partnership for the advancement of its member-unique needs regarding the social determinants of health. HBHCLF, a federally registered, non-partisan and non-profit organization was established on September 14, 2021, and was inaugurated on May 28th, 2022.

We are governed by a constitution and specific bylaws agreed to by our membership and represent the interest of Canadians of African heritage residing in Hamilton at the Municipal, Provincial and Federal levels on policy-oriented issues that impact them in areas such as housing, access to equitable and culturally-delivered healthcare, employment, and other social determinants of health.

Our mission

We seek inclusive health policy-making at all levels of government, advocating for equitable and culturally appropriate healthcare, housing, and employment opportunities for our community.

Aims

Our aim is to foster a strong, unified voice that promotes and advocates for the health and well-being of the African Diaspora in Hamilton.

Our goals

Partnerships

  • Seek to form partnerships with organizations to engage in Projects aimed at addressing issues related to social determinants of health within the African Diaspora in Hamilton.
  • Collaborate with organizations to work on existing policies and operations which negatively affect the overall health & well-being of the African Diaspora in Hamilton and request involvement in policy reviews, discussions and enactment to effect systemic changes.
  • Collaborate with other Black Organizations on Projects of mutual interest to achieve equity, diversity and inclusion for our communities. We will also share useful ideas, resources, data and information for our mutual benefit.

Research & Data Collection

  • Collect race-based data as a key step to improving health outcomes for African populations and addressing racism.
  • To build pictures of where race-based gaps, racism and racial bias exist in both Provincial and Municipal health, housing, employment, school, and Justice systems.
  • To paint a picture of the gaps and barriers in services and programs for African children, youth and families to effect changes to support their mental health and wellbeing.
  • To share data collected with local and provincial governments, public organizations, and agencies to make culturally effective interventions and policy changes.
  • To improve government funding allocations to Racialized and marginalized communities such as ours.

Community Engagement

  • Public Education through Town Hall meetings and Forums
  • Awareness Campaigns
  • Conferences
  • Presentations to both Public & Private Agencies
