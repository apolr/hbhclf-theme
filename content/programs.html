Africa Day

The Africa Day celebration in Hamilton, Ontario, is a vibrant annual event that unites the city’s African community and its supporters around May 25th, honoring African heritage and culture. This gathering features a rich array of activities, including cultural showcases of arts and crafts, thought-provoking lectures by community leaders, lively music and dance performances, and a culinary experience featuring authentic African cuisine. Organized by the Hamilton Black Health Community Leaders Forum, the celebration fosters unity among people of African descent, promotes cross-cultural understanding, empowers youth, and highlights the contributions of the African community to Hamilton’s diverse cultural landscape.

Ubuntu Seniors Program

The Ubuntu Seniors Program is an initiative of the Hamilton Black Health Community Leaders Forum created to celebrate, support, and engage seniors through meaningful social, recreational, and wellness-focused activities.

Sponsored by the Hamilton Community Foundation Martin Fund Grant and the Hamilton Urban Core Community Health Center, in partnership with NURU Hamilton, the program provides safe and joyful opportunities for seniors to connect, stay active, build friendships, and feel valued within the community.

From interactive gatherings to bowling at Splitsville Hamilton and the Seniors Tour of Niagara Falls, the Ubuntu Seniors Program reflects the spirit of community care, inclusion, dignity, and togetherness.

MentorAction

The Hamilton Black Health Community Leaders Forum is proud to partner with Interval House of Hamilton in their MentorAction program. This collaboration is dedicated to addressing gender-based violence within our community by providing specialized training to community members.

MentorAction is a program that empowers men to take an active role in ending gender-based violence. Through education, mentorship, and community involvement, the program challenges harmful societal norms and promotes a culture of respect and safety. By partnering with MentorAction, we aim to ensure that the African community in Hamilton is equipped with the knowledge and tools needed to recognize, prevent, and stand up against gender-based violence.

As part of this initiative, community members will receive comprehensive training on how to address and intervene in situations of gender-based violence, fostering a supportive and informed network within our community. Together, we are committed to creating a safer, violence-free environment for all.

New Horizon Seniors Project

The New Horizon Seniors Project, funded by the Canadian Government, is an initiative by the Hamilton Black Health Community Leaders Forum aimed at enhancing the well-being of seniors in our community. This program fosters social interaction and engagement among seniors, addressing the mental, physical, and psychological challenges they face. Through a variety of activities and support networks, the project seeks to alleviate isolation, promote health, and ensure that our seniors lead fulfilling lives within a supportive community.

Violence Against Women (VAW)

Since 2021, the Black Health Community Leaders Forum and Interval House of Hamilton have come together in a groundbreaking collaboration to better serve the African community in Hamilton. Together, they have established a dedicated position for an African Outreach Worker, specifically focused on providing Violence Against Women (VAW) support within the community. This initiative reflects their shared commitment to addressing the unique challenges faced by African women and ensuring they have access to the culturally sensitive resources and support they need. Through this partnership, they are making strides in creating a safer and more supportive environment for African women in Hamilton.

Hamilton Vaccine Ambassador Program

The Hamilton Black Health Community Leaders Forum (HBHCLF) is dedicated to becoming the unified voice for the African Diaspora communities in Hamilton, particularly regarding issues tied to the social determinants of health. The COVID-19 pandemic starkly highlighted the vulnerabilities within our community, exposing significant challenges that our members faced.

Many individuals in our community live in multigenerational households, often within confined apartment spaces, making self-isolation and quarantine efforts nearly impossible. Communication barriers further compounded these difficulties, particularly in accessing essential primary health care services. This lack of access, coupled with the stress of social isolation, anti-Black racism, and the mental health struggles many of our community members experience, led to increased anxiety, depression, and in some cases, suicidal thoughts.

A particularly pressing issue during the pandemic was the high level of vaccine hesitancy within our community. Deep-seated distrust in the public health system, fueled by historical injustices like the Tuskegee experiment, led to widespread fear and skepticism about the rapid development and safety of the COVID-19 vaccines. This lack of trust was exacerbated by the absence of African representation in decision-making bodies, leaving our community feeling marginalized and overlooked.

In response to these challenges, the HBHCLF partnered with Hamilton Urban Core Health Centre and Hamilton Public Health to address vaccine hesitancy among African, marginalized, and racialized communities in Hamilton. We proposed the Vaccine Ambassador Program, which was designed to build vaccine confidence through the efforts of trusted individuals within the community. These Vaccine Ambassadors were tasked with educating their peers about vaccine safety and efficacy, sharing personal experiences, and addressing concerns directly.

The program, enthusiastically supported by Hamilton Public Health, was successfully launched with the following primary goals:

Building Confidence

  • Promote community support for COVID-19 vaccination.
  • Distribute reliable information and resources about vaccines.
  • Act as a bridge between community members and necessary support services.

Building Trust

  • Partner with community leaders, organizations, and networks.
  • Provide guidance, support, and interpretation at vaccine clinics.
  • Offer accessible, informed answers to vaccine-related questions.

Sharing Information

  • Disseminate vaccine, health, and support information within the community.
  • Communicate community experiences to Hamilton Public Health.
  • Help shape Hamilton’s vaccine response based on community feedback.

On May 17, 2021, nine Vaccine Ambassadors were hired to launch this program, with additional ambassadors joining shortly after. Today, the team consists of 11 members who are deeply connected to various communities in Hamilton. They bring a diverse range of linguistic skills, speaking languages including Arabic, French, Nuer, Dazaga, Vietnamese, Mandarin, Spanish, Hindi, Rajasthani, Gujarati, and Punjabi.

The Vaccine Ambassadors have been thoroughly trained to engage with communities across the city and work closely with Hamilton Public Health’s Vaccine Confidence Team to stay informed and up-to-date. They also collaborate with the Vaccine Readiness Network to enhance their outreach efforts.

The Vaccine Ambassadors continue their mission to reach racialized and marginalized communities in Hamilton. If you are interested in collaborating with a Vaccine Ambassador for a community outreach event, please contact vaccineambassador@hamilton.ca.

For more information on building COVID-19 vaccine confidence, visit the City of Hamilton’s COVID-19 Vaccine Resource.
