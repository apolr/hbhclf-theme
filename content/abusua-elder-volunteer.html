Volunteer opportunity: ABUSUA/Elder Volunteer

It Takes a Village to Raise a Child

Supporting African/Black Families and Youth

  • Role type: Volunteer position with an honorarium
  • Program: It Takes a Village to Raise a Child
  • Location: Hamilton, Ontario; community, virtual and program locations as required
  • Reports to: Program Manager/Designate
  • Primary participants: African/Black parents, caregivers, families and youth

About the program

It Takes a Village to Raise a Child strengthens cultural connections, access to resources and community-based circles of care for African/Black families and youth who are involved with the Hamilton Child and Family Supports.

Role summary

The ABUSUA/Elder Volunteer is a respected community member who shares cultural knowledge, lived experience and guidance with parents, caregivers, families and youth. The volunteer facilitates parenting-in-the-diaspora sessions, culturally grounded circles and consultations; connects participants with supports; and promotes awareness of African/Black foster care through town halls and community engagement. The role supports family and youth well-being, cultural identity, belonging and collective responsibility. Volunteers receive an honorarium recognizing their time, knowledge and contribution.

Key responsibilities

Provide parenting information, cultural circles and consultation

  • Plan, facilitate or co-facilitate accessible, culturally responsive parenting-in-the-diaspora sessions for parents and caregivers.
  • Share practical knowledge and create respectful discussion about identity, belonging, intergenerational relationships, discipline, communication, education, child development and navigating Canadian systems.
  • Facilitate culturally appropriate or restorative circles where families and youth can speak, listen, reflect and seek guidance without judgment.
  • Offer cultural consultation on values, traditions, family structures and community practices while respecting diverse cultures, faiths, identities and perspectives.
  • Promote peer learning, family and youth voice, healing, resilience, cultural connection and belonging.

Desired qualifications and attributes

  • Trusted standing in, or a meaningful connection to, African, Black, racialized and/or newcomer communities.
  • Relevant lived experience, cultural knowledge or community wisdom related to parenting, caregiving, youth development or diaspora family life.
  • Experience supporting families or youth and contributing to groups, circles, information sessions, town halls or community gatherings.
  • Strong listening, communication and relationship-building skills, with empathy, patience, cultural humility and a non-judgmental approach.
  • Understanding of anti-Black racism, systemic barriers and migration-related challenges affecting the communities served.
  • Commitment to child and youth well-being, family voice, cultural identity and community-based care.
  • Reliability, teamwork, confidentiality and appropriate personal and professional boundaries.

Additional assets

  • Experience facilitating cultural, restorative, healing or community circles.
  • Knowledge of Hamilton services and African, Black, racialized or newcomer community networks.
  • Experience in foster or kinship care, child and family services, settlement, education, health or community development.
  • Fluency in a language spoken by the communities served.
  • Training in facilitation, mental-health first aid, conflict resolution, trauma-informed practice or crisis response.
  • Comfortable speaking at town halls and public education events.

Time commitment and honorarium

  • Flexible, part-time participation based on program and community needs, including sessions, circles, consultations, family support, meetings and town halls.
  • Some evening or weekend participation may be required.
  • Volunteers receive an honorarium; the program will communicate the amount, schedule and requirements. The honorarium does not create an employment relationship.
